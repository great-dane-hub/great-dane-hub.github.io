Flying With a Great Dane in the Cabin: BARK Air vs K9 Jets vs JSX vs Cargo Crate Compared

Size limits, real ticket prices and what the travel day looks like for a 110–175 lb dog, side by side, plus a checker that tells you which options your Dane actually qualifies for.

Home›Blog›Flying With a Great Dane: BARK Air vs K9 Jets vs JSX vs Cargo

Published: 2026-10-20 · Great Dane Hub

Flying with a Great Dane in the cabin used to be impossible unless your dog was a trained service animal. Three services have changed that: BARK Air, K9 Jets and JSX. The catch is that most guides compare them for a 40 lb doodle. A Great Dane is a different problem, because the breed standard puts an adult male at 140–175 lb. That one number rules out some options before price is even discussed. This guide compares all four routes, including the classic cargo crate, for a dog that is often heavier than the person holding the leash.

Quick answer

  • JSX is out for almost every adult Dane. Its in-cabin limit for medium and large dogs is under 80 lb, at least 30 lb below the lightest standard female.
  • BARK Air takes a Dane of any size. One ticket covers one dog and one human. The dog settles on the cabin floor beside you. 2026 European routes start at $7,850.
  • K9 Jets takes a Dane on one seat ("one pet over 51 lb"). Listed seat prices run from $7,925 to $13,850 depending on the route, and flights only go ahead once they are 75% sold.
  • Cargo is the only option on most commercial routes, and a Dane usually needs a giant or custom crate. Dog plus crate over 132 lb also triggers forklift spacers under the IATA crate rules.

Why a Great Dane changes the whole comparison

The AKC breed standard lists males at 30–32 inches at the shoulder and 140–175 lb, and females at 28–30 inches and 110–140 lb. Pet-travel limits are usually written around those exact thresholds, so the weight of your dog decides which column of the comparison you are even allowed to read.

Three things matter for a giant breed that barely register with a small dog:

  • Hard weight caps. Some airlines take large dogs in the cabin but cap them at a weight far below what an adult Dane weighs.
  • Floor space. On every cabin option the dog lies on the floor, not on a seat. A Dane lying flat takes up a full row's legroom, so seat position matters more than seat comfort.
  • Crate maths. In cargo, the crate is sized from the dog's body measurements. A tall dog needs a very large crate, and the combined weight brings in extra handling rules.

If your Dane is still growing, re-run the checker each month. A puppy that qualifies for JSX in spring may not qualify by autumn. The general growth and handling notes in our Great Dane care guides help you plan around that.

BARK Air with a Great Dane: how the day works

According to BARK Air's own FAQ, there are no size or breed restrictions. Each ticket covers one dog and one human, or two dogs if their combined weight is under 50 lb. That exception will never apply to a Dane. Crates are not allowed in the cabin. Dogs have no assigned seat and settle on the floor by their owner. If you want your dog to have a passenger seat of its own, BARK asks you to buy another ticket. The same FAQ says cabins usually carry 7–9 humans and 7–9 dogs, and that you should arrive 60 minutes before departure so the dogs can meet before boarding.

What the cabin means for a 150 lb dog

BARK plans boarding order and seating around the "dog dynamics" of each flight: size, temperament and social preferences gathered during an intake call after booking. For a Dane owner that call matters. Tell them plainly how much floor your dog needs when lying flat, whether your dog is comfortable with small dogs close by, and whether they settle better against a wall or bulkhead. Dogs must stay leashed on the tarmac, while taxiing, during take-off and landing, and whenever turbulence is expected.

Price and routes

In March 2026, Travel + Leisure reported four new European routes (Dublin and Stockholm from June, Athens and Berlin from July). Tickets start at $7,850 for one dog and one human. The new routes run as shared charters that only fly if enough passengers book. BARK's core scheduled network runs out of New York to Los Angeles, San Francisco, London, Paris, Madrid and Lisbon.

K9 Jets: one seat for one big dog

On its booking site, K9 Jets says passengers may travel with one pet over 51 lb, or two pets if each weighs under 50 lb. Pets stay by your side in the cabin for the whole flight. Route prices listed on the same page range from $7,925 to $13,850 per seat. The company says it has flown more than 10,000 passengers and pets since 2023, across the Atlantic and to the Middle East.

What a Dane owner actually reported

The most useful first-hand account comes from a May 2026 r/greatdanes thread by an owner who moved a Dane to Germany on K9 Jets. Their notes, which are user-reported rather than official, include:

  • K9 Jets asks for breed, weight and temperament at booking, then assigns you a seat or couch. You do not choose.
  • A body harness is required, and you must show the flight coordinator a muzzle before boarding, even if your dog never wears it.
  • Each person may bring checked bags up to 30 kg (66 lb) combined, plus one small personal item.
  • They booked in November for a May flight. The flight was not confirmed until roughly a month before departure, because both directions had to pass 75% sold.
  • Their flight carried 8 people, 7 dogs and 2 cats.

Commenters in that thread compared the seat (around $9,250 in the discussion) with the roughly $7,000 the owner had already been quoted to ship the dog in a crate. For a Dane, the gap between "cargo" and "in the cabin with you" turned out much smaller than most people assume.

The planning risk

The 75% rule is the biggest practical difference from BARK Air's scheduled flights. If you are relocating for a job with a fixed start date, build in a backup. That means a cargo booking you can cancel, or a second K9 Jets departure you could move to.

JSX: only an option for a Dane puppy

JSX's pet policy allows medium-to-large dogs in the cabin only if they weigh less than 80 lb. You buy an adjoining seat at the advertised rate, and the dog lies on the floor in front of it. Some other rules matter for a big dog:

  • One medium-to-large dog per customer, and a maximum of five pets in the cabin on any flight.
  • On aircraft with two seats on one side and one on the other, the dog may only use the floor in front of the window seat (C). The owner sits in the aisle seat (B), never in an emergency exit row.
  • The leash must be secured to the seat frame before the aircraft moves. Dogs are never allowed on the seats.
  • Disruptive behaviour, such as excessive barking, jumping on people or relieving themselves in the cabin, can get you and the dog denied travel.

Since the lightest standard female Dane is 110 lb, JSX only works for a puppy that is still under 80 lb, or for a smaller Dane mix. If that is you, the rules above are also the best low-cost rehearsal for a later BARK Air or K9 Jets trip. A short flight teaches your puppy to settle on a floor space next to strangers.

Cargo crate: how big a Great Dane crate really is

On commercial airlines, a dog too large for the cabin flies in the hold, in a crate built to the IATA Live Animals Regulations. The IATA Container Requirement 1 (Edition 52, January 2026) gives the minimum inside dimensions for one dog:

  • Length = A + ½B (A = nose tip to base of tail, B = ground to elbow)
  • Width = C × 2 (C = width across the shoulders or widest point)
  • Height = D + bedding (D = standing height to top of head or ear tip, whichever is higher)

A worked example

Take the example numbers pre-filled in the checker: A = 48 in, B = 17 in, C = 12 in, D = 38 in, with 2 in of bedding. The crate must measure at least 56.5 in long, 24 in wide and 40 in high inside. Crates this large are often custom-built. In the K9 Jets thread above, commenters compared the seat price with the cost of the owner's custom-made crate.

Preparing a 150 lb dog for a cabin flight

Every cabin option assumes a calm dog lying on the floor for hours, close to other dogs. You can train for that the same way you would for a long car trip or a busy vet waiting room. Work on it for weeks, not days.

  1. Mat settle. Teach a "place" or "settle" on a travel blanket the size of a row's legroom. Build up to 60 minutes with distractions around. K9 Jets asks owners to bring a blanket in place of a dog bed, so the blanket you train on can be the one you fly with.
  2. Short-leash tether. Practise lying calmly while the leash is clipped short to a fixed point, the way JSX and K9 Jets secure it to the seat frame or seatbelt.
  3. Harness and muzzle comfort. Even if your Dane will never wear the muzzle, being able to show it at boarding is a K9 Jets requirement. Getting your dog used to it removes one stress point on the day.
  4. Calm greetings. BARK Air and K9 Jets both put the dogs together for about an hour before boarding. Practise loose-leash meetings with small dogs, because a Dane bowing to play can scare a small dog's owner.
  5. Pack light. K9 Jets allows only one small personal item in the cabin, and BARK Air asks for a small carry-on. Treats, a collapsible bowl, wipes and puppy pads are enough.

Which option fits which Great Dane owner?

  • Transatlantic move, fixed start date: BARK Air on a scheduled route, with K9 Jets as a backup. Treat a shared charter as unconfirmed until the operator confirms it.
  • Transatlantic move, flexible dates: K9 Jets is worth checking for route coverage across the Atlantic and to the Middle East. Book early and expect late confirmation.
  • US coast to coast: BARK Air's New York to LA/SF flights are the only dog-first cabin option among these for a full-grown Dane. The other option is driving, which many Dane owners choose.
  • Puppy under 80 lb on a short US hop: JSX, with an adjoining seat. It doubles as flight training.
  • Route not served by any dog-first jet: cargo, with an IATA-compliant crate built from your dog's own measurements.

FAQ: flying with a Great Dane

Can a Great Dane fly in the cabin of a plane?

Yes, on dog-first services. BARK Air has no size limit, and K9 Jets accepts one pet over 51 lb per seat. JSX only accepts dogs under 80 lb, which excludes nearly all adult Danes.

How much does it cost to fly a Great Dane in the cabin?

BARK Air's 2026 European routes start at $7,850 for one dog and one human. K9 Jets lists seats from $7,925 to $13,850 depending on the route. Both prices include the owner's seat.

Does my Great Dane get its own seat on BARK Air?

No. Dogs lie on the cabin floor next to their owner. If you want a passenger seat for your dog, BARK Air asks you to buy an additional ticket.

What size crate does a Great Dane need for cargo?

Use the IATA formula: length = nose-to-tail-base + half the elbow height, width = twice the shoulder width, height = standing head height + bedding. Many adult Danes need roughly 56 × 24 × 40 inches inside or more, often a custom crate.

Why might a K9 Jets flight not be confirmed?

K9 Jets flights are confirmed once they reach 75% of seats sold. One Dane owner who booked in November for a May flight only got confirmation about a month before departure.

Can I fly with two Great Danes?

On BARK Air and K9 Jets, each large dog needs its own ticket or seat, and on JSX each customer may bring only one medium-to-large dog. In cargo, IATA requires dogs over 14 kg to travel in separate crates.

The short version

For flying with a Great Dane in the cabin, the breed's size narrows four options to two: BARK Air (any size, one dog and one human per ticket, from $7,850 on the 2026 Europe routes) and K9 Jets (one pet over 51 lb per seat, $7,925–$13,850 listed, confirmed at 75% sold). JSX stops at 80 lb, so it only suits puppies. Cargo remains the fallback on every other route, but a Dane-sized IATA crate is big, heavy and often custom-made. Run the checker with your own dog's measurements, then call the operator before you commit.
